# Code of Conduct and Grievance Procedure

## 1. Our standards

Everyone at Northwind Systems is expected to act with integrity, treat
colleagues and customers with respect, protect company and customer
information, and speak up when something looks wrong. The Code applies to
employees, contractors, and anyone acting on the company's behalf, including at
company events and on company communication channels.

## 2. Unacceptable behaviour

Harassment, discrimination, bullying, retaliation, violence or threats,
intoxication at work, falsification of records, theft, bribery, and the misuse
of confidential information are all unacceptable and may amount to gross
misconduct.

Protected characteristics include age, disability, gender identity and
expression, marital or civil partnership status, pregnancy and maternity, race,
religion or belief, sex, sexual orientation, and any characteristic protected by
local law.

## 3. Conflicts of interest

Declare any outside employment, directorship, significant shareholding in a
competitor, supplier relationship, or close personal relationship with someone
whose work you influence. Declarations are made in **PeopleHub → Compliance →
Declarations** and are reviewed annually. Declaring a conflict is not a problem;
failing to declare one is.

## 4. Gifts and hospitality

Gifts or hospitality with a value **over USD 100** must be declared and require
manager approval. Cash or cash equivalents must never be accepted. No gift may
be given or received where it could reasonably influence a business decision, and
nothing may be offered to a public official without Legal approval.

## 5. Raising a concern

Concerns can be raised in any of these ways:

1. **Line manager** — the fastest route for most issues.
2. **Skip-level manager or HR Business Partner** — if the manager is involved.
3. **`speak-up@northwind-example.com`** — reviewed by HR and Legal only.
4. **Anonymous whistleblowing line** — operated by an independent third party,
   available 24×7 in all working languages.

Retaliation against anyone who raises a concern in good faith is itself a
disciplinary offence.

## 6. Informal resolution

Where appropriate and safe, employees are encouraged to resolve issues directly
or with the help of a manager or mediator. Informal resolution is never required
for harassment or discrimination complaints.

## 7. Formal grievance procedure

| Stage | What happens | Timeline |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Submission | Written grievance to HR or the manager | — |
| 2. Acknowledgement | HR confirms receipt and names an investigator | 3 working days |
| 3. Investigation | Interviews and evidence gathering; the employee may be accompanied by a colleague | 10 working days (extendable with notice) |
| 4. Outcome | Written outcome with findings and any actions | 5 working days after investigation |
| 5. Appeal | Written appeal to an uninvolved senior manager | 10 working days from the outcome |

Investigations are confidential; information is shared only with those who need
it. Where an allegation is serious, the employee concerned may be suspended on
full pay as a neutral act while the investigation proceeds.

## 8. Disciplinary outcomes

Possible outcomes are: no action, informal guidance, written warning, final
written warning, or dismissal. Gross misconduct may lead to dismissal without
notice. Warnings expire after 12 months unless stated otherwise.

## 9. Data privacy in investigations

Grievance and disciplinary records are held for the period required by local
law and are accessible only to HR, Legal and the decision makers. AI tools —
including the Enterprise Knowledge Copilot — are never used to make or
recommend disciplinary decisions.
